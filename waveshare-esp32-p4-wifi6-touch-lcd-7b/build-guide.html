The two variants

Side sections, to scale

Both share the same front facade, display mounting, clamp tabs and back-panel USB-C port. They differ in where the speaker goes. The chin speaker sounds best because it points at you. The compact one is about a third smaller in depth and height. Its duct carries the sound under the display to a slot grille in the front lip.

Before you start

Check two things

Not yet confirmed by a print

USB-C socket cutout. It's a 14.1 × 5.75 mm rounded rectangle (R1.3) in the 2.4 mm back wall, from your measurements. The clips are rated for a 2.5 mm panel, and a slightly thinner wall is fine for snap clips.

Speaker standoffs. They're Ø7.5 mm and sit in the speaker's corner notches. The notches need to be at least 8 mm across.

The glass pocket and window are already confirmed with the facade test frame. If you're printing in a new material or on another printer, print p4_7b_facade_test_frame first. It takes a few minutes. Press the glass in from behind and check for an even sliver of black glass around the screen.

Assembly

Put it together

Work with the front plate lying face-down on a soft cloth. Tick each step off as you go; your progress is remembered in this browser.

  1. Mount the speaker on the plate

    Set the speaker into the ring on the back of the plate, cones toward the grille. Each corner ear drops over a standoff spigot. Fix each ear with an M3 × 8 and a washer.

    4 × M3 × 8 pan4 × washer

  2. Seat the display in the pocket

    Lower the display into the pocket from behind, glass first, until it rests against the facade. The wider black border goes to the bottom, toward the speaker chin.

  3. Fit the four clamp tabs

    At each corner, lay a tab across the display's standoff and the column next to it. Drive an M2.5 × 6 into the standoff, then an M3 × 8 through the slot into the column. The tabs are identical, because the slot covers both column spacings. Snug them up evenly so the glass sits flat.

    4 × clamp tab4 × M2.5 × 64 × M3 × 8 pan

  4. Connect the speaker

    Plug the speaker lead into the PH2.0 SPK header at the top edge of the board. The lead needs about 12 cm to get there.

  5. Install the USB-C socket

    Feed the socket's wires through the cutout in the back wall from outside, then push the socket in until its clips snap behind the wall. Splice its wires to the right-angle cable (see Wiring the USB-C port).

    USB-C socket

  6. Plug in the right-angle cable

    Plug its head into the board's middle USB port, on the left edge seen from the front, with the ribbon pointing back toward the body.

  7. Close the enclosure

    Lower the plate onto the body. The top cap lands on the back wall, with its rib just inside it. Fit the four countersunk M3 × 8 through the side walls into the plate's bosses.

    4 × M3 × 8 countersunk

  8. Add the feet

    Press the rubber feet into the four recesses under the base.

Wiring

Wiring the USB-C port

The socket comes with four wires ending in a 2.0 mm-pitch PH2.0 plug, in the order V, D−, D+, G. The board's only USB input is its USB-C port, so splice the socket's wires to the right-angle cable that plugs into it. Cut that cable to length, leaving enough slack to reach the back wall. Join wire to matching wire, solder, and cover each joint with heat-shrink.

V5 V (VBUS)red
D−Data −white
D+Data +blue or green
GGroundblack
Don't plug the PH2.0 plug into the board

It looks exactly like the board's 4-pin I2C, UART, CAN and RS485 headers, but those carry signals, not USB. The socket's V wire would put 5 V where it doesn't belong.

Check before you power it

Wire colours aren't standard across cables. Confirm each wire with a multimeter in continuity mode, against the plug pins, before soldering. With nothing connected, check there's no continuity between V and G.

The socket carries USB 2.0, which covers everything the board's middle port uses: power, flashing and serial logs. Some USB-C sockets with only four wires lack the 5.1 kΩ resistors a USB-C charger looks for. If a C-to-C cable doesn't power it, use a USB-A to USB-C cable.

Good to know

After it's closed

Power switchIt ends up inside the case. Leave it on before closing up.
RESET / BOOTThese are inside too. Flashing over USB normally works without them.
MicrophonesThe two mics face into the case. A 7-hole cluster in the bezel under each one lets sound in.
DuctThe speaker's sound stays inside the duct and comes out a 98 × 14 mm mouth, behind 17 slots in the front lip. That keeps the case from buzzing and keeps the speaker away from the mics.
VentsVertical slots near the top of the back wall let warm air out.
